## Q2. Does pausing mask an existing failure?

**Yes, baseline `d430d781` can mask recorded failure in ordinary state readers.** Its holder branch precedes `hostHasNvidiaSmi`; it preserves device/process/sample history, refreshes `attemptAt` and `sampledAt`, keeps the older observation in `lastProbeAt`, and explicitly removes `unresponsiveSince`, `stallEvidence`, and `why`. It does not increment missed probes. See [gpu-sampler.ts, lines 488–531](src/gpu-sampler.ts#L488-L531) at that baseline. Thus a held-slot cycle need not notice an already blocked `nvidia-smi` and can erase a prior stall marker. The brief's paused holder 81471 plus simultaneous D-state callers is consistent with this weakness, but no 09:20 state JSON was supplied to prove the exact historical field transition. The later repair is recorded separately in Q4.

Freshness readers accept a fresh paused `sampledAt` and can return the preserved last sample; [gpu-samples.ts, lines 146–173](src/gpu-samples.ts#L146-L173) and [gpu-visibility.ts, lines 20–31](src/gpu-visibility.ts#L20-L31). Fresh owner state therefore does not establish fresh device telemetry or a responsive adapter. Existing [tests, lines 175–194](tests/gpu-sampler.test.ts#L175-L194) explicitly expect clearing the stall and a successful cached read; these tests were inspected, not executed.

There is an independent safeguard: the read-only `--check` path inventories `/proc`, reports D tasks with dxg wait channels even while paused, and checks sampled-process PIDs against zombies; [gpu-sampler.ts, lines 343–463](src/gpu-sampler.ts#L343-L463). Its wording “GPU-holding zombie” means an intersection with the last sampled PID list, not proof of present driver resource ownership. The notification path only reacts to recorded `unresponsiveSince`; [gpu-stall-notify.ts, lines 16–26 and 97–125](src/gpu-stall-notify.ts#L16-L26). A cleared/missing marker can therefore yield “no new stall” without examining dxg tasks. This is a detection/mitigation weakness, not evidence that it initiated the GPU fault.

## Q6. Is the admission queue adapter serialization? What support claim is warranted?

**No.** Published baseline `d430d781` declares one admitted GPU job (`gpu_max_concurrent_jobs=1`): [baseline resource-policy.toml, lines 1–4](../agent-resource-run/resource-policy.toml#L1-L4). The subsequently inspected **root working tree**, however, declares **6** in [/home/fuyu/dotfiles/tools/agent-resource-run/resource-policy.toml, line 4](/home/fuyu/dotfiles/tools/agent-resource-run/resource-policy.toml:4); the coordinator identifies this as an unrelated unpublished change. Both observations must be retained. The runner reads `AGENT_RESOURCE_POLICY` when supplied, otherwise the policy file adjacent to its executing source ([runner, lines 139–145](../agent-resource-run/src/agent-resource-run.ts#L139-L145)). A new launch using that root source without an override therefore reads the working-tree policy, not a policy pinned to a published commit. Neither baseline policy 1 nor the later working-tree value 6 proves the effective policy of the historical incident or every current runner; those launch-specific paths/environment and their read times were not captured. Neither policy was changed by this investigation.

`flock` acquisition queues cooperating launchers; waiting is bounded by `min(900, walltime_seconds)` per lock and reports code 75 on expiry: [baseline runner, lines 323–367 and 575–644](../agent-resource-run/src/agent-resource-run.ts#L575-L644). That is admission exclusion for participating commands, not a GPU driver lock or a demonstrated strict-FIFO/fairness guarantee. Policy 1 serializes participating admitted job scopes; policy 6 permits multiple admitted scopes subject to the separate declared-VRAM check. Neither setting serializes every GPU/driver caller. Multiple lock stages and cleanup mean that bound is not a guaranteed total completion deadline.

The sampler takes a separate owner lock and observes research slot ownership without holding the same slot across its probe. An already-started probe, an unmanaged caller, or the interval after inspection can overlap an admitted job. [Sampler operations, lines 75–78](GPU-SAMPLER.md) explicitly limits the protocol to participating queries. The old paused holder 81471 and the Julia scope/parent chain must not be equated without their historical lock/cgroup captures. The missing `slots.toml` found during inspection is an optional **Julia compile-capacity** declaration ([runner, lines 167–185](../agent-resource-run/src/agent-resource-run.ts#L167-L185)), not evidence that GPU policy was missing; policy defaults to the adjacent `resource-policy.toml` unless overridden (lines 139–142). Historical override values were not captured.
