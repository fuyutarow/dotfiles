# Delivery — render the evidence state without outrunning it

> **SOLE owner** of genre-shaped output, section-level substance, visual artifacts, gap language,
> final audit presentation, stop-writing conditions, and living-update behavior. It consumes the
> review plan, corpus records, and synthesis claims; it does not re-run retrieval or synthesis.

## 1. Lead with the answer and its license

Open with:

1. the decision question;
2. the bounded position;
3. the `coverage contract`;
4. the strongest reason to believe the position;
5. the most decision-relevant uncertainty.

Do not begin with a paper-by-paper chronology unless historical development is the question.
Organize the body by claims, constructs, mechanisms, comparisons, or decision branches. Papers are
evidence nodes, not section headings.

## 2. Shape the deliverable to the review mode

| Review mode | Required shape |
|---|---|
| Closed-corpus synthesis | supplied-corpus scope, claim clusters, evidence table, discrepancies, bounded conclusion, missing external search |
| Rapid/bounded review | decision brief, exact search boundary, evidence summary, limitations caused by shortcuts |
| Systematic review / SLR | protocol/registration, methods, applicable reporting checklist, study/result flow, appraisal, synthesis, certainty/limitations |
| Scoping review / map | map dimensions, corpus distribution, descriptive findings, evidence gaps, limits on inference |
| Realist/explanatory synthesis | programme theory, context–mechanism–outcome configurations, evidence roles, refinements and residuals |
| Critical review / SoK | explicit contribution type, evaluation/contextualization of prior work, supporting synthesis, counterargument, field consequence |

For a venue SoK, state which contribution is being made: new viewpoint, evidence challenging or
supporting a belief, taxonomy, or the venue’s current alternative. A taxonomy is not mandatory when
another contribution carries the paper.

## 3. Claim resolution

Every load-bearing claim should expose:

- the exact proposition or quantity;
- the scope in which it is licensed;
- source-claim identifiers and/or direct-source locators;
- quantitative magnitude and uncertainty when the claim is quantitative;
- mechanism only when an explanatory/causal claim and evidence support it;
- assessment status, basis, and limitations.

Descriptive claims do not need invented mechanisms. Qualitative and formal claims do not need fake
numeric magnitude. A specific locator and bounded proposition are universal; the other fields are
claim-type dependent.

Use a compact audit table:

```text
section | job in the argument | load-bearing synthesis claims |
source path | uncertainty/counterevidence | action if weak
```

Contribution sections should teach the intended expert something not already available from one
source. Methods, limitations, and background sections are judged by whether they perform their job,
not by a forced “surprise.”

## 4. Visual and tabular artifacts

Use the minimum set that makes the reasoning inspectable:

- forest/effect plot for comparable estimates;
- harvest/effect-direction or structured result display for a justified non-meta synthesis;
- evidence map for corpus distribution;
- taxonomy or matrix when classification is the contribution;
- claim/evidence graph for provenance and discrepancy structure;
- context–mechanism–outcome table for explanatory synthesis.

No universal “exactly one hero figure” rule applies. One strong artifact is often useful; several
non-redundant artifacts may be necessary. Each visual must have a decision it enables, sourceable
cells/marks, legends, and the same scope as the prose.

## 5. State gaps without manufacturing priorities

Classify what is missing:

| Gap type | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Coverage gap | relevant population, setting, language, period, or source type was not searched or represented |
| Evidence gap | eligible work has no or insufficient evidence for a defined question |
| Inconsistency gap | aligned evidence remains materially discrepant |
| Comparability gap | measures, estimands, designs, or reporting prevent a valid synthesis |
| Construct/theory gap | concepts or mechanisms are under-specified or competing |
| Method gap | available designs cannot identify the quantity or mechanism of interest |
| Translation gap | evidence exists but deployment, external validation, or implementation is absent |

For each gap:

```text
unknown | evidence that it is a gap | consequence | evidence/design needed |
current obstacle | coverage caveat
```

An empty taxonomy cell or no-hit query is only a gap candidate. Check feasibility, alternate
terminology, value, and corpus coverage first.

Importance, tractability, and stakeholder value are judgments, not properties extracted from the
literature alone. Prioritize gaps only when the user asks for an agenda; expose who supplied the
values and assumptions.

## 6. Calibrated conclusion language

Use the claim ledger status directly:

| Status | Safe language |
|---|---|
| `supported` | “Within `<scope>`, the evidence supports C.” |
| `supported-with-limitations` | “The evidence supports C within `<scope>`, limited by L.” |
| `uncertain` | “The corpus does not resolve C because L; evidence E would discriminate.” |
| `not-comparable` | “The studies do not estimate the same quantity under the current definitions.” |
| `unsupported` | “The current corpus does not support C.” |

Do not rewrite `unsupported` as “not-C is established.” Do not replace `uncertain` with a
speculative flip condition. If a demonstrated boundary exists, state it with its evidence.

## 7. Adversarial review

Before delivery:

1. walk every load-bearing synthesis claim through its full path to exact source loci;
2. verify all bibliographic and priority/“first” claims against primary records;
3. confirm that the strongest in-scope counterexample was searched or inspected;
4. inspect excluded and `not-decisive` evidence that could change the position;
5. challenge method applicability, comparability, independence, and value judgments;
6. compare prose strength with claim-ledger assessment status;
7. confirm that flow counts derive from corpus units, not claim rows;
8. list unresolved findings instead of editing them into apparent closure.

Run prose or document-structure skills only after evidence judgments are stable:
`structuring-documents` may reorganize the artifact; `arguing-research-papers` may position a
manuscript’s governing claim. Neither replaces this evidence audit.

## 8. Stop-writing gate

Stop when:

- the review plan and `coverage contract` are visible;
- every load-bearing conclusion resolves through checked claims and/or direct-source loci;
- the operator and appraisal method are applicable and named;
- discrepancies and missing information keep their calibrated states;
- counterevidence and defensible sensitivity analyses are adjudicated;
- visuals/tables have a decision purpose and provenance;
- limitations describe how the coverage and method constrain use.

Do not require every claim to have a moderator, GRADE label, flip condition, taxonomy cell, or
figure. Those requirements are conditional branches.

## 9. Living updates

Preserve stable corpus, result, and claim identifiers. On an update:

1. rerun retrieval from the last search date under the recorded protocol;
2. link new reports to existing studies/artifacts;
3. extract only new or changed results;
4. re-run affected appraisal and synthesis claims;
5. record conclusions whose status or scope changed;
6. regenerate derived counts and visuals.

Version naming belongs to the host project. Do not route to an uninstalled document-discipline
skill or invent a collection-wide version scheme here.
